## Research focus

The survey distinguishes two roles of artificial intelligence in integrated sensing, communication, and computation (ISCC). In **AI-aided ISCC**, AI methods support the estimation, optimization, and control of integrated system functions. In **AI-oriented ISCC**, sensing, communication, and computation are designed to support AI workloads, including distributed learning and inference. These roles are distinct but not mutually exclusive.

## AI–ISCC co-design

Their intersection is the central focus of the survey. **AI–ISCC co-design** extends joint system design beyond sensing, communication, and computation resources to include AI/model and representation variables. Examples include feature representations and quantization, model partitioning, aggregation, and inference-mode selection. The survey examines which of these variables are actually coupled in existing approaches and how that coupling is evaluated in relation to AI task performance.

The survey also develops a **closed-loop perspective**, in which feedback from model state, uncertainty, or task performance informs subsequent sensing, communication, computation, and model decisions.

## Literature synthesis

The reviewed work is organized by the role of AI, the variables jointly designed, the objectives and evaluation metrics, and the evidence supporting the claimed integration. Representative settings include federated edge learning, distributed inference, and multimodal edge perception. This analysis distinguishes substantive joint system–model design from approaches that merely deploy AI workloads on ISCC infrastructure.
