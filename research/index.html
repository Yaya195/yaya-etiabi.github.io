Research

Research directions

My work examines machine learning for wireless systems, with a current focus on learning and inference at the network edge and the joint design of sensing, communication and computation.

Edge Intelligence & ISCC

Learning and inference in wireless networks depend on how observations are acquired, transmitted and processed, as well as on the communication, computation and energy resources available across devices and edge infrastructure. My current research considers these functions jointly within integrated sensing, communication and computation (ISCC).

Topics I investigate include communication-efficient learning and device-edge inference. I am also exploring Neuromorphic ISCC as a broader research direction. Initial investigations include spiking models and event-driven processing, but this direction is not restricted to any single model family.

Wireless Sensing & Localization

My work in this area uses radio and inertial observations for indoor localization and examines how wireless measurements can support the estimation of propagation geometry.

Recent studies address joint delay and angle estimation from MIMO-OFDM observations and radio-based simultaneous localization and mapping using estimated multipath parameters. In parallel, LocLLM investigates language-model-based radio-inertial localization and adaptation across buildings.

Federated and Adaptive Learning

My doctoral research addressed learning for wireless localization when measurements are distributed across devices and environments. It includes federated meta-learning for adaptation to new localization tasks and federated distillation to reduce information exchange during collaborative model training.

These studies combine method development with quantitative evaluation of localization error, adaptation and communication overhead.

Research methods

Across these topics, I formulate learning and estimation problems, implement models and compare them with established baselines. The evaluations include simulation, experimental datasets, quantitative error analysis and, where relevant, measurements of communication and computational cost.
